## Three reporting constraints the frozen data imposes

**1. Do NOT compare the two completeness numbers directly.** Llama graded 3
probes, Phi graded 4 — different probe sets, because Llama's `current_holder`
was excluded by the competence gate. "44.2% vs 71.7%" is not a like-for-like
comparison and must not be presented as one.

**2. Llama fails the primary task at n=120.** Its `current_holder` competence
is 46.7%, i.e. it cannot reliably answer "who has the X?" on these items. At
n=24 it was 88%; the larger, freshly-resampled sample revealed the smaller
run was optimistic. Llama therefore supports the SELECTIVITY claim (where its
two graded NO-probes both have 100% baseline competence) but NOT the
completeness claim.

**3. Phi is the primary result, Llama is corroboration.** Phi has 97-100%
competence on all four graded probes and a clean completeness/selectivity
dissociation. Frame the paper on Phi and report Llama as replication of the
selectivity failure specifically.

## Wording constraints

- Do NOT write "competence amplifies the failure." Two models cannot support
  `competence ↑ ⇒ selectivity failure ↑`. Write instead: *the selectivity
  failure cannot be explained by poor baseline task competence — it persists
  in Phi despite 99-100% baseline accuracy on all graded probes.*
- Do NOT claim the cross-question diagnostic is novel. RAVEL (ACL 2024,
  arXiv:2402.17700) establishes effectiveness-vs-selectivity for static
  entity attributes; MIB (ICML 2025, arXiv:2504.13151) reports that
  full-vector baselines fail RAVEL by changing unrelated attributes. Both are
  direct antecedents and must be cited as such in the introduction.
- The contribution is the EXTENSION: from static entity attributes to a
  dynamically computed sequential state, under a progressively strengthened
  control stack.
- Do NOT imply DAS or MIB patch arbitrary final-answer states. DAS restricts
  to `[CLS]` for NLI; MIB brute-forces manually selected token locations.
